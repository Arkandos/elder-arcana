# Belief

Faeries are a reflection of kith and their imagination. What kith believe, faeries can become. Stories and misconceptions take on a life of its own as faeries coalesce to make it true. Faeries exist to play a role in a story like actors in a play. Most are [[#Cognizance|incognizant]] and thus unaware of their own self. Like the most proficient method actor, they fully believe they are their role.

The role can be almost anything. A terrible beast to scare children, a brave knight that dies in battle, or a princess to be rescued. The role is played to [[#Purpose|draw energy]] from kith.

As part of their role, they have a taboo. Something acts or objects that according to the story they must avoid at all costs or have their powers nullified. Those knowledgeable about faeries can wield the power of their taboos against them. Each faerie has an individual taboo so what works against one has no effect against another.
# Purpose
Faeries exist to draw upon the imagination and emotion of [[Kith and Kin|Kith]], feeding off it. These experiences are something faeries leech off to power and improve themselves. However this relation is not inherently harmful to kith. The faeries do not steal or take anything from them but harvest this radiating energy that would otherwise go to waste.

However to create situations where such energies can be most effectively harvested, faeries orchestrate stories. To evoke emotions and encourage creativity that they then can gorge on. These stories can often be harmful to the mortal actors of the play, as the stakes must be real to create the right circumstances.
# Cognizance
Most faeries are unaware that they are faeries. They are called incognizant, for they do not know why they act in certain ways, why they do things certain ways or even that they are actors in a play they themselves orchestrate unconsciously.

Some faeries are cognizant of their own existence and why they want the vitality of kith. The level of cognizance varies, but as it increases the overall power of the faerie seems to decrease. Generally only the weakest of faeries are fully cognizant.

The [[#Glamour|glamours]] that make up their body seem to act as an anchor in many ways. The stronger they are, the greater their glamour. But as the glamour grows stronger their cognizance shrinks as they sink deeper into the role they have chosen. 
# Glamour
Faeries are not real in the same way most other creatures of the cosmos are. Their bodies are glamours, a special type of magic which is like an illusion made real. 

Faeries true form is a formless spirit, detectable only by those who can see incorporeal spirits. When a faerie 'dies', it is their body of glamours that is broken and falls dead to the ground. The spirit is detached from the body, and begins travelling back to the Faerie plane to reform a new body. Only if this bodyless spirit is destroyed does the faerie truly die.

The time it takes a faerie to form a new body varies, influenced both by the power of the faerie and its [[#Cognizance]]. An incognizant faerie can reform almost instantaneously if its role requires it. A fully cognizant faerie can take a very long time to reform, and once they do they may very well lose both personality and memories. It is as if cognizance is a defect, and Faerie tries to correct it when they return.

Thus cognizant faeries fear death because while their spirit may survive, their being will not.
# Improving
Faeries are incredibly mutable creatures able to change into almost any form. The greater their [[#Glamour]] the more varied and powerful forms they can undertake. They use the [[#Purpose|energy]] harvested from kith to improve upon their glamours allowing them to perform more wondrous feats than before.

However this comes with a cost. The glamour works as an anchor against the changes that would normally improve a being. They have great difficulty learning things as kith would. They do not have the spark of creativity required to create things on their own. You could teach a faerie to sing a song, but not how to make a new one. You could teach a faerie a story, but it would not learn how to make one up.
