The town of Crucible is the largest settlement in the Quru desert, an outpost founded in O.C. 620 by \<X\> to exploit the newfound resources of the desert. Named the Crucible as it has become a melting pot for cultures and different interests.  
Crucible was founded by the governor as a place to project power from over the area, and to funnel essence from the desert back home to \<X\>. The location was chosen as it is easily defendable, on top of ancient ruins with a source of water essence to sustain the colony. An artificial river flows in a circular pattern around the Kinriton Arcanists tower before being dispersed in the area around the town. The area around Crucible is being terraformed and is currently arable land being farmed for food. Anything that cannot be produced locally is imported through the long trade routes, driving up prices for many goods and sundries.

While originally the area was micromanaged by the governor, his focus has shifted. Most matters these days are delegated (including law enforcement). The hunt for essence is both what keeps Crucible going, and what allows them to expand. Without the essence of earth and water nothing would survive out there. The many cultures and interests create friction as their agendas oppose each other. The governor’s forces do not step in unless their goals are threatened, but the \<lawmen\> keep a heavy hand on law and order.

The population of Crucible started rapidly expanding in 625, as it started pulling people from near and far. The region is quickly growing, faster than can be sustained. Many die on the journey to Crucible, and many more once they get there. The governor has no vested interest in supporting settlements other than their own, so food can often be an issue outside of Crucible’s bounty.

While prices are high, and dangers abound, it is a place of opportunity. Weapons, armor, adventuring gear are always cheap. Either subsidised by the guilds, or sold as scraps from… less fortunate groups. At least those who sell recycled gear say their owners met unfortunate ends.

And who’s ever met an untruthful salesman?
